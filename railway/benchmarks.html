The honest ceiling. On In-the-Wild — deliberately hard, adversarial, real-world deepfakes — we catch roughly 6 in 10 at a false-alarm rate under 7%. That is far ahead of commodity detection (which catches almost none) and far short of perfect. It's why Sonave is designed as a second factor: the verdict feeds a wire-hold and a re-authentication callback, it doesn't replace them. Any vendor claiming 99%+ on adversarial real-world audio is quoting a lab.

Methodology

  • Model under test: the exact checkpoint serving production (sonave-xlsr-meet, XLS-R + SLS head, fine-tuned on meeting-codec audio) — not a lab build.
  • Unseen means unseen: the 27 commercial tools and all In-the-Wild content are absent from training data.
  • Meeting-audio pipeline: test clips are passed through Opus compression at meeting bitrates before scoring, mirroring what our bot receives from a live call.
  • Operating point: the same thresholds production uses — 0.40 (SUSPECT) / 0.70 (FAKE), with a wire-hold requiring three consecutive red windows.
  • Live scoring: 8-second rolling windows per speaker, first verdict after ~4 s of speech, re-scored every 4 s; windows without sufficient voiced audio are never scored.
  • Regression gate: every retrain must beat this table within tolerance before deployment; these numbers update only when the deployed model does.

What these numbers do not mean

  • They are not a guarantee about any individual verdict — detection is probabilistic, in both directions.
  • They do not cover video or image deepfakes. Sonave is voice, in meetings, on purpose.
  • They will change — attacks improve, and so do our models. The date stamp above is part of the claim.

Why we publish

Fraud teams have been burned by accuracy marketing. If a vendor won't tell you their catch rate on tools outside their training set, through a meeting codec, at a stated false-alarm rate — they're telling you a lab story. We'd rather you know exactly what you're buying, because the teams that compare honestly are the teams we win.
