QUESTIONS

Straight answers.

Everything you need to know about how Sonave intercepts synthetic voice attacks.

Can a deepfake voice be detected on a live Google Meet, Zoom or Teams call?
Yes. Sonave sends a visible bot into the meeting that streams each speaker's audio to a detection model trained specifically on meeting-codec audio. Every speaker gets a REAL / SUSPECT / FAKE verdict about 4 seconds after they start talking, re-scored every 4 seconds for the rest of the call.
How accurate is deepfake voice detection, really?
On 27 unseen commercial voice-clone tools played through meeting audio, Sonave catches 97% where a commodity detector catches 2%. On the hardest public real-world deepfakes (In-the-Wild), it catches about 57% at a 94%+ real-voice accuracy — which is why verdicts are designed as a second factor next to callbacks, not a replacement. Full methodology at usesonave.com/benchmarks.
Does everyone on the call need to install something?
No. Only the person protecting the meeting signs in; the bot joins as a normal, visible participant. Nobody else installs, clicks, or configures anything on their laptop or phone.
What is a wire hold?
When a voice scores in the red band for three consecutive windows, Sonave fires an automated webhook into your payment-approval flow to pause a wire transfer pending secondary out-of-band re-authentication. It also produces an exportable forensic report containing scores, timestamps, and audio evidence.
What happens to my meeting audio?
Audio is processed in volatile memory to produce verdicts. Voiceprints are stored solely as mathematical vector embeddings that you can delete in one click. We never sell data or share meeting audio between customers — full details at usesonave.com/privacy.
How much does Sonave cost?
Free for 5 monitored hours every month — no card required. After that it's $8 per monitored hour, billed by the minute, only while the bot is actually in a call. No seats, no tiers.
